## Biology of Joy
### Dopamine
Feeling of power and rewarding fun from playing well.
Composing new music and creating.
### Serotonin
Serenity from finishing a piece, calming.

### Oxytocin
Music with others: Singing, GGT, etc.

### Endorphins
Excitement from playing an organ piece, nervousness from performing.

### Music and Biology of Joy
- Title card, right before pedal entrance as background music
- Opens to Cm Fugue on organ in the pedal entrance
- Voiceover: Hi, I'm Christopher Gilbert, and playing music helps to give me a biology of joy.
- Back to organ for a short duration.
- Narration (me on the organ bench): Finishing memorizing for a new song, or reaching a new milestone in a hard one feels extremely rewarding. The pipe organ specifically gives me amazing feelings of power and even fun, all of which help to boost my dopamine levels.
- Cut to GGT Video
- Voiceover: Music can also be intense and even nerve-wracking sometimes, especially during performances. The potential to mess up badly in front of so many people is terrifying, and it pumps my brain full of endorphins which cause feelings of stress or adrenaline. Even so, that makes the feeling after a great performance all the better.
- Narration: Music is athletic: it requires fine control of many parts of your body, and you have to spend a lot of time training your muscle memory to become better.
- Voiceover of Pedal Solo: Playing a virtuosic piece on the piano, or singing a solo in choir, or speeding through a pedal part on the organ can leave me sweating from effort, and gets my endorphin level up.
- Cut to Dubois on the piano video
- Voiceover: Some songs are less energetic, and instead give me feelings of serenity related to the neurotransmitter serotonin. Whenever I need to calm down, I turn to this type of music to give me a sense of peace and quiet unlike almost anything else I do.
- (Let Dubois piece finish)
### Music and Natural Highs
- Play my composition while narrating:
- Playing music is my natural high, and I often use it to regulate my emotions. If I'm nervous or angry, I might play something quiet to feel calm and content. If I'm just tired, I might play something more upbeat to help wake myself up. If I'm having a bad day, I can sit at the piano playing or composing music for hours, slowly feeling better as I focus more and more on the music and enter a flow state.
- Through the biology it creates and the mindset it fosters, it helps me to feel joy.
